Lesson 1 of 5
Signal vs. Noise:
Choosing Clarity
The single mindset shift that changes everything about how you use AI.
⏱ Approx. 8 minutes
Meet Marcus

Marcus is a marketing director at a tech company. He has 12 AI tools open. He added a 13th one last Tuesday. Three different AI writing assistants sit in his browser — one for social, one for email, one for "everything else." None sound like him. His content is inconsistent. His team is confused. He's spending more time managing AI than thinking.

What is Marcus missing? It's not another tool. It's a clear signal before the tool is opened.

The core principle

AI is not intelligent. It's a prediction engine. It takes everything humanity has ever written and predicts what word comes next based on the pattern of what you gave it.

"AI amplifies you. It doesn't replace you. If your thinking is clear, your output will be clear. If your thinking is chaotic, AI will amplify your chaos."

— Dr. Eric Goodman, AI Clarity Challenge

This is the coherence principle: your mental state, your intent, and the quality of your thinking directly shape the quality of what comes out. Most people ask "which tool should I use?" The better question — the one that changes everything — is: what am I actually trying to do?

The 3 orientation questions

Before you open any AI tool, ask yourself these three questions:

  1. What problem am I actually trying to solve? Not "what do I want to create" — what is the real decision or challenge?
  2. How can AI specifically help me achieve this goal? Not "how do I use AI" — how does it serve THIS specific goal?
  3. How does this align with my values and strengths? What part of this can only I — as a human — do?
The 10-80-10 framework
10%
You
Vision. Intent. Your 3 questions. This is your job. No one else can do it.
80%
AI
Heavy lifting. Writing, structuring, researching, drafting. Let it work.
10%
You Again
Human review. Fact-check. Refine. Does this sound like me? Own the output.

Most people skip the first 10%. That's why the output never matches their intent.

Why this matters

Research shows that when teams adopt AI with clarity-first practices, they see up to 60% less decision fatigue, 3x faster tool adoption, and 80% higher confidence. The tool doesn't change — the approach does.

This week's action

Before your next AI interaction, write down your 3 answers on a sticky note. Just try it once. Notice what's different about the output you get.

The Pre-AI Pause
3 questions. 1 breath. Everything changes.
⏱ Approx. 8 minutes
Marcus, 4 weeks later

Marcus did something different today. Before opening any tool, he took a breath. A real one — not the kind you take while already typing. He picked up a sticky note and wrote three things: the decision he was actually making, the outcome he was responsible for, and his role in the process.

Then he opened one tool. His content manager read the result and said: "This finally sounds like you."

What changed wasn't the tool. What changed was what Marcus brought to it.

The pause practice — step by step
  1. Take 2 slow breaths. Breathe in fully. Exhale completely. Your nervous system resets. Your thinking clears. This isn't soft science — tension shows up in every word you type.
  2. Q1: What decision am I actually making? Not "what content do I need." What is the real choice? The more specific, the better your output.
  3. Q2: What is the outcome I am responsible for? The why behind the task. Not "I need a social post." Why? What should happen because of it?
  4. Q3: What is MY role here? What part can only a human — specifically you — do? AI can write. It cannot have your values, your relationships, or your accountability.
Try it right now

Think of an AI task you have coming up this week. Write your 3-question answers in the space below. You'll use them in Lesson 4 when you build your first structured prompt.

Your 3 answers
Why the breath matters

When you're stressed, your body is in fight-or-flight mode. Your thinking narrows. Your prompts get vague. That state shows up in your output. You wouldn't send an important email mid-panic attack. Don't prompt in one either.

Pro tip

Set a "10-second pause rule": before every AI session, write your 3 answers before typing a single prompt. People who do this consistently report fewer revisions and much less AI frustration overall.

Your Voice Is
Your Superpower
Stop sounding like a robot. Start sounding like you.
⏱ Approx. 10 minutes
The problem: AI drift

You write a prompt. You get output. It's technically correct. But it doesn't sound like you. It sounds like it was written by a very confident robot who read everything on the internet.

This is called AI drift — and it happens when AI doesn't know who it's speaking as. Most frustration with AI isn't about the tool. The tool is plenty smart. The problem is identity. AI doesn't know who you are unless you tell it.

The 4 dimensions of your voice
VOICE
How you sound on the page
Formal or conversational? Complex sentences or short punchy ones? Industry jargon or plain talk? This is your sonic fingerprint.
TONE
Your attitude toward topics
Curious? Assertive? Warm? Skeptical? Tone is the emotional layer underneath the words. It's what makes someone feel like they're reading a specific person.
STYLE
Your signature language patterns
Metaphors? Opening with questions? "Here's the thing..." Do you tend to lead with data or with story? Those patterns are your style.
STRUCTURE
How you architect ideas
Do you build to a conclusion or lead with the punchline? End with a call to action or an open question? Structure is how you frame and deliver ideas.
Build your voice style guide — 4 steps
  1. Gather 2–3 writing samples. Emails, blog posts, social posts — anything you've actually written. Even a few paragraphs work. The more representative, the better.
  2. Paste this analysis prompt into your AI tool of choice along with your samples.
  3. Review the output. Read it back: does it sound like you? Adjust anything that's off. When you read it and think "yes, that's me," it's ready.
  4. Save your Voice Style Guide. Copy it into a note or your AI's memory. From now on, every important prompt starts with it.
Your voice analysis prompt — copy this
PASTE INTO AI
Analyze the writing samples below and identify my:
1. Voice — how I sound (formal/casual, complex/plain)
2. Tone — my attitude toward topics (curious, assertive, warm, etc.)
3. Style — signature patterns, metaphors, opening phrases
4. Structure — how I organize and deliver ideas

Then create a Voice Style Guide I can paste into future prompts
so AI outputs sound like me, not like generic AI.

[paste your writing samples here]
Why this is the most valuable thing you'll build

Once you have your Voice Style Guide, you paste it at the top of every important prompt. Your output immediately stops sounding generic and starts sounding like you wrote it — because it knows who you are.

Pro tip

Don't have much writing yet? Use a voice recording. Transcribe a few minutes of yourself talking through a work problem. AI will analyze your spoken patterns just as well as written ones.

Lesson 4 of 5
Prompting That
Actually Works
9 principles. One framework. Results that sound like you.
⏱ Approx. 10 minutes
The difference one prompt makes
Weak prompt

"Write a marketing email for our product launch."

Missing: role, context, audience, length, format, voice, constraints. Result: generic noise.

Strong prompt

"Act as head of marketing for a B2B SaaS company. Write a 200-word launch email for HR managers at companies with 50–200 employees. Warm but direct tone. No exclamation points. Use the voice guide below."

The 9 principles — click each to expand
Talk Like a Human

You don't need formal command language. Conversational context gets better results.

❌ "Compose a professional business development correspondence."
✅ "I need to pitch to investors next week. What's the most compelling way to present our growth metrics?"
Tip: If you'd say it to a smart colleague over coffee, you can say it to AI.
Be Direct

Vague requests produce vague results. Tell AI exactly what you want.

❌ "Help me with marketing."
✅ "Write a 3-email drip sequence for inbound leads who haven't booked a call in 14 days."
Tip: If you can't be direct, you don't know what you want yet — use the Pre-AI Pause first.
Give Context

Who are you? What's the situation? What are the stakes? The more AI knows, the better it performs.

❌ "Write a product description."
✅ "I'm the CEO of a startup. Our product helps solo consultants automate client onboarding. Target buyer: overworked solopreneurs who hate admin."
Tip: Context is the job brief. No brief = guesswork. Good brief = great output.
Specify the Output

Table? Email? Bullet list? 200 words? Under 150 characters? Tell it the exact format — always.

❌ "Give me some ideas." (How many? What format? How long?)
✅ "Give me 5 subject line options. Under 50 characters each. Curious, not salesy."
Tip: If a form has a character limit, tell AI that limit. It will honor it.
Share Examples

Show AI what "good" looks like before you ask for it. Examples calibrate the output instantly.

❌ "Write an email in my style." (What's your style?)
✅ "Here's an email I sent last week that I love. Match this tone and rhythm: [paste example]"
Tip: One good example is worth 10 adjectives trying to describe what you want.
Iterate — Push for Better

Never accept the first output. Always push back. AI responds to challenge.

❌ Getting output, thinking "good enough," and moving on.
✅ "That's a 7. What would you change to make it a 10?" or "That's mediocre. What's missing? Try again."
Tip: Rate responses 1–10 and ask AI to improve its own score. The jump from 7 to 10 is usually significant.
Use Branching

When you want to try a different direction, don't start over. Go back up the thread and branch — try a new path without losing your work.

❌ Starting a brand new conversation every time you want to explore a different angle.
✅ Going back up the chat to an earlier point and clicking "edit" to try a completely different direction from that moment.
Tip: Think of your conversation like a tree. You can climb back up and grow a different branch anytime.
Chunk It

Big projects need stages. Don't ask AI to write a complete business plan in one prompt. Break it down.

❌ "Write me a complete go-to-market plan for my product launch."
✅ Step 1: Research. → Step 2: Structure. → Step 3: Key sections one at a time.
Tip: AI is like a brilliant assistant — give it one clear task at a time and it excels. Pile everything in and it overwhelms.
Attach Your Voice Guide ← Most Important

For any output that goes into the world with your name on it, start with your Voice Style Guide. Every time. No exceptions.

❌ "Write me a LinkedIn post about AI trends."
✅ "Using the following Voice Style Guide: [your guide]. Write me a LinkedIn post about AI trends. 150 words. End with a question."
Tip: This is the single step that separates generic AI content from content people recognize as genuinely yours.
Your structured prompt template
TEMPLATE
ROLE: Act as [expert role relevant to this task]
CONTEXT: I am [who you are]. The situation is [brief context]. The goal is [specific outcome].
TASK: [Exactly what you want — be direct]
OUTPUT: [Format: email / list / table / paragraphs]. [Length: X words / X items]. [Constraints: no jargon / no bullet points / etc.]
VOICE: [Paste your Voice Style Guide here]
Why structure beats creativity

A creative prompt without structure gets creative-but-wrong results. A structured prompt tells AI exactly what to do and who to be. The output is predictably good — not just occasionally good.

Start with 3

Don't try to apply all 9 principles at once. Start with: Be Direct, Give Context, and Attach Your Voice Guide. Those three alone will transform your outputs immediately.

Lesson 5 of 5
The 10-80-10 Loop:
Iterate Like a Pro
Bring it all together. Own the output.
⏱ Approx. 9 minutes
The complete workflow
  1. Pause. Breath + 3 questions. Know your intent before you open anything.
  2. Attach your Voice Style Guide. Every important prompt, every time.
  3. Write a structured prompt. Role + Context + Task + Output format + Constraints + Voice guide.
  4. Review and challenge. When output arrives, ask: "What assumptions did you make?" Fix any that are wrong before you build on them.
  5. Iterate. "That's a 7. What would make it a 10?" Keep pushing until it's right.
  6. Final human review. The last 10%. You look at this with your eyes, your judgment, your values. Verify facts. Make sure it sounds like you. You own the output.
3 power moves most people never use
01 — Ask for Assumptions
After getting any important output, say: "List the assumptions you made to produce this." At least one will be wrong. Fix it before building on a faulty foundation.
02 — Improve Your Own Prompt
Before submitting: "How could this prompt be improved so you can give me better output?" Let AI help you ask better questions. Then rewrite it.
03 — Let AI Interview You
"What 5 questions do you need answered from me to give a world-class response?" Answer the questions. Then you'll have a great prompt without guessing.
Quick wins — pick 2 to do this week
Win #1

Rewrite one email using a structured prompt and your Voice Style Guide. Compare it to the original. Notice the difference.

Win #2

Next AI output you get — ask it to list its assumptions. See what it says. Correct any that are wrong.

Win #3

Build your Voice Style Guide today using the prompt from Lesson 3. Save it somewhere accessible before you do anything else in AI.

Win #4

Run the Pre-AI Pause before every AI interaction this week — breath + 3 questions, every time. One week of this habit changes how you use AI permanently.
